## Claim

The chapter classifies business processes along five axes: organizational vs.
operational vs. implemented (a granularity/abstraction ladder, Fig 1.6), intra-
organizational vs. choreography (see [[orchestration-vs-choreography]]), degree of
automation, degree of repetition, and degree of structuring. These are independent
dimensions — a single real process has a position on *every* axis at once (e.g., an
insurance claim is: operational-level, intraorganizational, mixed automation, high
repetition, but low structuring — Leymann & Roller's "production workflow" sits at the
opposite corner: structured and highly repetitive).

## Why it matters to a viewer

The natural (wrong) instinct is to draw this as a taxonomy tree — a process "is" one
type or another. The book's own examples resist that: airline e-ticketing is fully
automated *and* highly repetitive *and* structured, but an insurance claim is partly
manual *and* frequent *and* still fairly structured, while a shipbuilding project is
manual, one-off, and deliberately unstructured (a "collaborative business process"
where the goal shifts from efficiency to traceability). Seeing these as independent
sliders rather than categories is what lets a reader correctly place *their own*
process instead of forcing it into the nearest labelled bucket.
