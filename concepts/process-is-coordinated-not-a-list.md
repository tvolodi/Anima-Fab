## Claim

A business process is not just a list of tasks — it's a *coordinated, goal-directed*
set of activities with defined inputs and outputs. Hammer & Champy anchor it on
input/output (activities that "take one or more kinds of input and create an output
that is of value to the customer"); Davenport adds ordering and cross-boundary reach
("a specific ordering of work activities across time and place... across or between
organizational subunits"). The book's own definition fuses both: activities performed
*in coordination*, jointly realizing a business goal, enacted by a single organization.

## Why it matters to a viewer

This is the foundational reframe the whole book depends on. Most people's mental model
of "how work gets done here" is a checklist or an org chart. A process view says: the
unit that matters is not the task and not the department, it's the *coordinated whole*
that produces something a customer values. Without this reframe, later ideas (models
vs. instances, orchestration vs. choreography, the lifecycle) have nothing to attach
to — they all presuppose the viewer already sees "the process" as a first-class thing.
