# Episode 1 — "Your Company Has a Process. Nobody Has Seen It."

**Series:** BPM, honestly
**Audience:** RU / CIS
**Runtime target:** 3:15–3:30
**Teaches:** nothing notational. Establishes *why* modelling exists.
**Production cost:** low — four simple flow diagrams, one overlay, one wipe.

**Scenario:** приём сотрудника — onboarding a new hire.

---

## The argument this episode makes

> The parts of your company that get audited are already modelled — precisely, and by
> someone who cannot afford to be wrong. The parts nobody audits aren't modelled at all.
> Onboarding crosses that line four times in three days.

This is the CIS-specific version of the point, and it is stronger than the Western one.
In a company where the tax service will fine you a couple of monthly payrolls over a
one-kopeck discrepancy, accounting and HR document flow are *rigorous*. That rigour is
externally enforced. Nobody enforces anything on the space between departments.

So this episode is **not** "everyone is sloppy." It is: **two people are exactly right,
and the process still fails.** That's a more interesting claim and a truer one.

---

## Cast (voice)

- **NARRATOR** — the series voice. Dry, unhurried, slightly amused. Never sells anything.
- **OLGA** — HR. Precise, slightly defensive, knows the Trudovoy Kodeks deadlines cold.
  She is not a fool and the script must never imply she is.
- **SERGEY** — IT. Tired. Speaks in things that have already happened to him.
- **DIRECTOR** — management. Speaks in principles he has never tested.
- **THE NEW HIRE** — one line, at the end. Should sound genuinely uncertain.

Four voices. If using TTS, pick clearly different timbres — the episode depends on the
audience tracking who is speaking without being told. **Test Russian TTS quality before
committing**; if it's poor, record Olga and Sergey with real people. Their lines are the
episode.

Format: audio left, screen right. Timings are targets, not law.

**The sameness is the joke.** Vary anything and the overlay lands weaker.

### ACT 2 — THE OVERLAY — 1:45–2:25

The centre of the episode, and the only visually complex moment.

| Audio | Screen |
|---|---|
| **NARRATOR:** Four people. One process. | The four columns sit side by side, all visible — three diagrams and one blank. |
| **NARRATOR:** Let's put them on top of each other. | The four slide toward centre and **overlay**. Blue, amber, green, and nothing. Two seconds. Don't rush the move. |
| *(no narration — 2 full seconds)* | The result is a mess. Arrows crossing. Three different starting points, misaligned. Sergey's from-nowhere arrow now visibly points into the gap between Olga's diagram and the empty fourth. **Let the silence hold.** |
| **NARRATOR:** Nobody here is lying. | Overlay holds. Slight pulse on each colour in turn as it's named. |
| **NARRATOR:** Olga is describing what the law requires. Sergey is describing what he can do when someone tells him. The director is describing a document from 2019. | Each colour brightens on its clause, returns. |
| **NARRATOR:** And the manager is describing nothing at all, because he doesn't know there's anything to describe. | The empty fourth region pulses — an absence pulsing. |
| **NARRATOR:** All of them are right. That's the problem. | Hold on the mess. |
| **NARRATOR:** Olga's part is audited, so Olga's part works. Nobody audits the space between Olga and Sergey. | The blue diagram's verified border glows briefly. Then the *gap* between blue and amber highlights — empty space, no boxes in it. |
| **NARRATOR:** That space is where the new guy sits for three days. | The gap stays lit. Everything else dims. |

> **Production note:** the overlay must be genuinely ugly. If it composes nicely, the
> episode fails. Deliberately misalign the start events 20–40px vertically, let arrows
> cross. Ugliness is the argument.
>
> The *gap* — the empty region between the diagrams — is the hero of this act.
> Compose the four so there is a real visual hole in the middle.

## Recording notes

- **Record voice first.** Animate to the recorded track. Never the reverse.
- **Olga must not sound like a bureaucrat joke.** She's the most competent person in the
  episode. Slightly defensive, because she's used to being blamed for delays that aren't
  hers. Recruit a real person for this if TTS can't carry it.
- **Sergey is tired, not angry.** Everything he describes has happened to him repeatedly.
  The flat repetition of "If I know in advance" is the performance.
- **Director: short lines, slightly too loud, total confidence.**
- **The new hire's single line should sound genuinely uncertain** — not comic. It's the
  emotional close of the episode.
- Narrator never raises pitch at line ends. No enthusiasm. The material is funny because
  it is delivered as fact.

## Silences that must survive the edit

Four, and they are the episode:

1. After the opening dot appears — before "Somebody knows how that happens."
2. **Two seconds on the empty fourth column** (Act 1). The most important silence.
3. Two full seconds on the overlay mess, before "Nobody here is lying."
4. 1.5 seconds on the half-drawn arrow at "Wait — who tells IT?"

Every editing instinct will say to trim these. Don't.

## Music & sound

- No music until Act 2. Enters *under* the overlay, low, pulled at "Nobody here is lying."
- Returns at Act 4's cut to white — the only warm moment in the episode.
- SFX: soft low click per box appearing. One tick per token step. Nothing else.
  Resist whooshes.
- **Silence on the empty fourth column.** No sound at all. Not even room tone if you can
  manage it.

## Terminology decisions needed before Episode 2

Episode 1 avoids BPMN vocabulary entirely, so nothing is locked in yet. Episode 2 teaches
notation and cannot avoid it. Decide and stay consistent:

- *gateway* → шлюз / развилка / or keep English?
- *event* → событие (probably safe)
- *pool / lane* → пул / дорожка?
- *task* → задача / операция?

Practitioner usage is split, and mixing registers mid-series reads as amateur. Worth
picking once and writing down.
